Atomic AI Training · Session 1

Working With Claude

Twenty minutes from now, Claude will be reading your own files. Three minutes from now, you'll know why that matters.

Before the demo

There are five ways to work with AI. Most people only know the first.

You don't need to know how any of it is built. You do need to know which rung you're standing on — because the gap between rung one and rung four is where all the value sits.

  1. 01

    Chat

    You ask, it answers. Genuinely useful — but it starts from nothing every time, and it has never seen your business.

  2. 02

    Context

    It can see what you actually work with. The answers stop being generic.

  3. 03

    Connect

    It reaches into where your work already lives, with your permission. Now it knows what happened yesterday.

  4. 04

    Delegate

    You stop asking and start assigning. The same work happens the same way, without you starting it.

  5. 05

    Compound

    What one person worked out becomes how the team works. Here the returns stop being linear.

Most leaders we meet are at stage one, and assume that's the whole thing.

So here's what stages two through five actually look like.

Six steps. About twenty minutes. You'll do all of it on your own laptop, with your own files — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

The six steps

1 Chat → Context2 min

Claude moves in

Right now Claude probably lives in a browser tab. It's helpful, but it's a stranger — it can't see anything you actually work with. The desktop app changes the address.

Your demand plan is a spreadsheet sitting on your laptop. A browser tab can't open it. The app can.

Browser tab

Where's my demand plan drifting this month?

I can't open files on your computer from here. If you paste the numbers in, I'll take a look at them.

2 Context4 min

Claude gets a desk

Cowork is where Claude does work that touches your files. You point it at folders. That's the whole idea — and it's the most important thing to understand today.

Folders, not uploads. You're not sending anything anywhere — Claude reads the files where they already sit, only while the app is open, and only in the folders you point it at.

Cowork — connected folders
What Claude can do now Read last month's deck and draft this month's first pass.

Nothing was uploaded. Nothing left your laptop. Claude just knows where things are now.

3 Connect3 min

Claude gets reach

A connector is just permission — it lets Claude look at your inbox or your calendar the way a new analyst would, with your say-so and nothing more.

You can switch a connector off as easily as you switched it on. Claude only ever sees what your own login can see.

Cowork

What should I worry about this week?

I don't have visibility into your inbox or calendar, so I can only talk in general terms. Typically you'd look at supplier confirmations, POs past their promise date, and whatever's on the S&OP agenda.

Flip both switches and watch the answer stop being advice and start being your week.

4 Delegate6 min

Claude gets a habit

You just did something useful. A skill is you telling Claude do that again, the same way, every morning, without me asking.

You don't write code for this. You do the thing once, then ask Claude to remember how you did it.

That's the shift. You stopped asking and started assigning.

5 Delegate4 min

It becomes a thing you keep

The briefing is words. Sometimes you want a surface instead — something you and your team open, that's current every time you look at it.

6 CompoundOptional · usually Session 2

It compounds

Everything so far was you. The step after is your team — the briefing you built becomes theirs, pointed at their own patch.

This is where it stops being a personal productivity trick and starts being how the team works. It's also where you'll want a little structure around who owns what — which is the conversation for Session 2.

After week one

Three things will have changed

Your morning starts differently You don't open the day by triaging an inbox. The triage is already done and waiting in six lines.
One report writes its own first draft Whichever recurring thing you most resent producing. You edit it now instead of building it.
The question changes You stop asking what can it do and start asking what should I hand it. That's the whole shift.

This is the foundation. From here it splits two ways — deeper for you, as the work you hand over gets bigger, and outward to your team, as what you worked out stops being yours alone.
