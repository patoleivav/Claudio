Working With Claude

In twenty minutes, Claude will be reading your own files.

Before the demo

Five ways to work with AI. Most people only know the first.

The gap between the first and the fourth is where the value sits.

  1. 01ChatYou ask, it answers. Useful, but it starts from nothing every time.
  2. 02ContextIt can see what you work with. Answers stop being generic.
  3. 03ConnectIt reaches your inbox and calendar, with your permission.
  4. 04DelegateYou stop asking and start assigning. Work happens without you.
  5. 05CompoundWhat one person worked out becomes how the team works.

Here is what stages two to five look like.

Six steps, about twenty minutes. Your own laptop, your own files, nothing uploaded anywhere.

12 min

Setting Claude on Desktop

Claude in a browser tab cannot see your files. The desktop app can.

Your demand plan is a spreadsheet on your laptop. A browser tab cannot open it.

Browser tab

Where is my demand plan drifting this month?

I cannot open files on your computer from here. Paste the numbers in and I will take a look.

24 min

Cowork and Context

Cowork is where Claude works with your files. You point it at folders. That is the whole idea.

Folders, not uploads. Claude reads files where they already sit, only while the app is open.

Connected folders
What Claude can do nowRead last month's deck and draft this month's first pass.
33 min

Your first connectors

A connector is just permission. It lets Claude see your inbox and calendar, the way a new analyst would.

Flip both switches and watch the answer stop being advice and start being your week.

Cowork

What should I worry about this week?

I cannot see your inbox or calendar, so I can only talk in general terms. Usually you would check supplier confirmations, POs past their promise date, and the S&OP agenda.

46 min

Routines are skills

You did something useful once. A skill is telling Claude to do it again every morning, without being asked.

No code. You do it once, then ask Claude to remember how.

54 min

Artifacts

The briefing is words. An artifact is a page you and your team open, current every time you look at it.

Involve your team!

Everything so far was you. Next it is your team. Your briefing becomes theirs, pointed at their own patch.

That is where you will want some structure around who owns what.

After week one

Three things will have changed

Your morning starts differently The triage is already done and waiting in six lines.
One report writes its own draft You edit it now instead of building it.
The question changes You stop asking what it can do and start asking what to hand it.

This is the foundation. From here it splits two ways. Deeper for you, as the work you hand over gets bigger. Outward to your team, as what you worked out stops being yours alone.
